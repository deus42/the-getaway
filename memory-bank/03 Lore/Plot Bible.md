# Plot Bible

This document is the canonical narrative context for The Getaway. It defines what is true in the world and how Level 0 connects to the larger campaign. Gameplay rules belong in [[01 MVP/Game Design]] and the per-system specifications; unresolved narrative facts are explicit in [[01 MVP/14 Specification Review Queue]].

## 1. Global setting — 2036

The United States is ruled by Chancellor Victor Harrow through the Emergent Security Directorate (ESD), a fusion of privatized police, militia power, intelligence systems, and corporate logistics. Public institutions still use the language of safety, continuity, and civic order while food, identity, movement, utilities, and information are increasingly conditional.

The Atlantic coast is repeatedly damaged by subtropical storms. Seawalls, pumps, and public infrastructure fail unevenly. Ration tokens, local scrip, and corporate credit systems coexist. Cities are contained through cameras, biometric checkpoints, predictive systems, public-private security, and curated information rather than a single visible occupying army.

The North Atlantic Resistance Coalition (NARC)—Canadian, European-remnant, and sympathetic Latin American interests—supports covert resistance and evacuation without committing to an open invasion.

## 2. Core conflict — Operation Cold Iron

Harrow is preparing Operation Cold Iron: a rapid northern campaign intended to seize Canadian hydroelectric capacity, rare-earth supply, and strategic infrastructure before domestic collapse becomes impossible to conceal.

Cold Iron depends on more than soldiers. It requires:

- identity and population-control systems;
- predictive surveillance and behavioral analysis;
- secure freight, weapons, and fuel logistics;
- corporate data centers and communications;
- civilian narratives that recast aggression as emergency stabilization.

The campaign asks whether the player can expose, fracture, or stop that system without reproducing its disregard for ordinary people. The protagonist’s missing father and the evidence discovered across the campaign make Cold Iron both political and personal.

## 3. Tokyo prologue — Hidzu-controlled civic life

Level 0 takes place outdoors in Tokyo before the Miami campaign begins.

Hidzu Corporation publicly presents itself as a guarantor of safety, transit reliability, efficient services, and a bright technological future. In practice it controls identity infrastructure, surveillance, logistics access, public information, and the conditions under which a person remains socially legible.

Tokyo is not a war zone. Its threat comes from normality under observation:

- cameras treated as civic furniture;
- algorithmic identity checks;
- transit credentials and controlled access;
- public screens that merge service information with propaganda;
- drones that verify people flagged by the network;
- laptops, terminals, and social feeds that make institutional judgment feel ambient;
- citizens adapting their behavior because they may be observed.

Takahiro Kobayashi is the public executive authority associated with Hidzu’s Tokyo control system. His exact formal title remains `OPEN-NAR-003`; writing may refer to him as a Hidzu executive or authority until it is approved.

Hidzu secretly supplies surveillance AI, identity infrastructure, and logistics capability used by Harrow’s Operation Cold Iron. Eisenclave remains Harrow’s primary American integrator and operator. This distinction is canonical:

- **Hidzu:** upstream technology, identity, and logistics supplier;
- **Eisenclave:** American collaborator that integrates and operates those systems for Harrow and the ESD.

## 4. Protagonist

The protagonist is an American expatriate living in or moving through Hidzu-controlled Tokyo. They are not a fixed operative, soldier, courier archetype, or mandatory character named Trace.

At New Game, the player chooses:

- a callsign;
- one of four authored appearances;
- a grounded set of attributes and skills.

The protagonist has touched evidence connected to their missing father and Operation Cold Iron. That contact caused Hidzu’s systems to flag them. Their Tokyo chronology and the exact flagging evidence remain `OPEN-NAR-001` and `OPEN-NAR-002` and must be resolved before the opening script is frozen.

What is already true:

- the protagonist is exposed and cannot simply resume ordinary life;
- they have limited leverage and no unexplained military competence;
- their father disappeared during the Battle of Miami;
- credible evidence suggests he may have survived and entered ESD custody or transfer routes connected to Cold Iron;
- the protagonist’s immediate escape points toward Miami, which becomes the first full campaign level.

## 5. George

George is the protagonist’s private AI companion. Only the protagonist perceives his floating AR presence.

George is useful because he can organize verified facts, compare known risks, explain blocked actions, and preserve context under pressure. He is not omniscient, magical, or an autonomous hacker. He cannot invent evidence, reveal undiscovered surveillance, operate terminals, move the player, or guarantee uncertain outcomes.

George’s physical/technical substrate—implant, wearable, lens, phone-linked system, or another form—remains `OPEN-NAR-009`. Until resolved, scripts should describe his private AR presence without asserting unsupported hardware.

George’s voice is precise, dry, observant, and honest about uncertainty. His humor should relieve pressure without turning fear into a gag.

## 6. Level 0 cast

### Lira

Lira is the primary mission contact. Hidzu confiscated medical supplies she needs returned. In exchange for the protagonist’s help, she can arrange an outbound transit credential toward Miami.

Lira is not a quest vending machine or generic trust meter. Her briefing establishes the human need, the logistics-site problem, the midnight deadline, and the promised passage. Her debrief responds to what the protagonist actually did.

Her precise identity/relationship, the medkit beneficiaries, and the confiscation event remain `OPEN-NAR-004` through `OPEN-NAR-006`.

### Naila

Naila is an optional contact with systems knowledge and factual context about Hidzu’s camera network, connected terminal topology, and shipping information. She grants knowledge, not a hacking buff.

Her approved biography, personal stakes, and knowledge provenance remain `OPEN-NAR-010`.

### Brant

Brant is an optional contact who understands service routes, delivery timing, and credible public behavior around the logistics site. He grants practical knowledge, not a stealth multiplier.

His approved biography, role in Tokyo, and knowledge provenance remain `OPEN-NAR-011`.

### Hidzu security and civilians

Hidzu security should feel procedural and institutionally confident rather than theatrical or monstrous. Civilians are not decoration: they demonstrate how ordinary people queue, work, wait, comply, avoid attention, and create credible blending contexts.

The exact number, identities, and schedules of Level 0 security and civilians remain content-authoring decisions in [[01 MVP/14 Specification Review Queue]].

### Takahiro Kobayashi

Kobayashi appears in propaganda, civic messaging, or broadcast material rather than as an in-person Level 0 boss. His presence connects polished public promises to the surveillance system surrounding the protagonist.

## 7. Level 0 narrative contract

### Opening

1. The player creates a protagonist.
2. The protagonist begins at 18:30 inside the outdoor safehouse boundary.
3. George introduces only the immediate danger, controls, and known situation.
4. The protagonist meets Lira within the first meaningful story decision window.
5. Lira explains the confiscated medkits, the Hidzu logistics site, the midnight deadline, and the promised outbound passage.

### Preparation

Naila and Brant are optional. Consulting them changes what the protagonist knows and how precisely routes and systems can be understood. Skipping both must remain viable.

### Infiltration

- **Dusk:** public/service activity makes delivery behavior and blending relevant.
- **Curfew:** reduced public activity makes camera topology, service access, discrete hiding, and the verifier drone more important.

The routes are not moral alignment choices or character classes. They are different readings of the same watched city.

### Cache and evidence

The protagonist explicitly recovers the confiscated medical supplies. A nearby shipping manifest may reveal the Hidzu–Harrow/Cold Iron connection. Naila’s designated fact guarantees that recognition; otherwise an authored Awareness check may recognize it. Failure or omission does not block the medkit objective.

The precise beneficiaries of the supplies, seizure incident, facility identity, and manifest contents remain open narrative facts and must be frozen before final dialogue/content authoring. Facility identity is `OPEN-NAR-015`.

### Escape and handoff

The protagonist must resolve active surveillance rather than cross an invisible completion trigger. They explicitly return the medkits to Lira, receive the transit credential, return to the safehouse, and validate passage before midnight.

The exact fictional reason for the midnight deadline remains `OPEN-NAR-007`; mechanically it is fixed and must receive a credible narrative explanation.

### Debrief

Lira and George respond to the factual outcome ledger:

- contacts consulted;
- route timing;
- camera loop and trace;
- hiding/blending recovery;
- pursuit or interception;
- injuries and Paranoia peak;
- medkits returned;
- manifest recognized, missed, or not inspected;
- transit validated.

The optional manifest changes the future Miami handoff state. Miami does not load until a real Level 1 exists.

## 8. Level 0 themes

### Escape is social as well as physical

A person leaves because another person takes a risk, a system has a gap, and the protagonist understands enough to act. Escape is not a magical door unlock.

### Surveillance changes behavior before it uses force

The system’s power is visible in the way citizens wait, speak, route themselves, and manage identity. Cameras and drones matter because the city already believes them.

### Paranoia is honest stress

Paranoia is the protagonist’s physiological and cognitive stress under observation. It never falsifies evidence, invents threats, or lies through the UI.

### Knowledge creates leverage

Facts change practical options, but no single contact or build owns the only valid route. Understanding is valuable because it clarifies risk and consequence.

### Modern life is the mirror

Public feeds, terminals, laptops, identity systems, corporate safety language, suppressed missing-person stories, and algorithmic suspicion should feel like an extrapolation of current life—not occult technology or generic cyberpunk spectacle.

## 9. Campaign continuation

Level 0 is a prologue. It adds the Tokyo/Hidzu supply-chain layer without replacing the established campaign spine.

### Level 1 / Act 1 — Miami

The protagonist reaches Miami and encounters Shelterline, the local grassroots resistance led by Amara Velez. Miami must not repeat the Level 0 medkit-recovery opening; its first operation should use the Tokyo evidence and transit consequence to begin a distinct stage of the campaign.

Miami establishes:

- the aftermath of the Battle of Miami;
- Harrow/ESD control in the United States;
- Shelterline’s civilian evacuation and port-logistics work;
- evidence of northbound Cold Iron materiel;
- a credible lead concerning the protagonist’s father.

The exact playable Miami opening is Post-MVP design work.

### Act 2 — Mid-Atlantic Corridor

The protagonist moves through refugee routes and damaged infrastructure, encounters Eisenclave’s operational role, and seeks NARC extraction or support while factions disagree about acceptable costs.

### Act 3 — New Columbia

The campaign reaches the former Washington, D.C., now a controlled corporate-political center. The protagonist exploits fractures inside the ESD and must decide how and when to expose Cold Iron evidence.

### Act 4 — Northern Breakpoint

The protagonist reaches Cold Iron staging infrastructure near the Great Lakes and works to prevent the invasion. Endings should reflect accumulated factual and human consequences, not a generic reputation meter.

This structure is directional campaign canon, not a promise that every older quest hook or system remains approved.

## 10. Power factions

### Emergent Security Directorate (ESD)

Harrow’s principal coercive institution. It combines public authority, contracted enforcement, intelligence, and military preparation. Internal tension exists between ideological loyalists, career administrators, and profit-driven corporate partners.

### North Atlantic Resistance Coalition (NARC)

An external coalition that supplies intelligence, routes, and limited support while prioritizing long-term destabilization and geopolitical risk management over open intervention.

### Shelterline

A Miami grassroots network led by Amara Velez, focused on civilian survival, port disruption, and covert evacuation. Shelterline’s authority comes from work and trust expressed through authored consequences, not a generic meter.

### Eisenclave Conglomerate

Harrow’s American corporate integrator for surveillance, utilities, data analysis, and drone/security operations. Eisenclave turns upstream systems—including Hidzu capability—into ESD operational power.

### Hidzu Corporation

Tokyo-centered corporate supplier of identity, surveillance AI, and logistics infrastructure. Hidzu’s public language is optimistic, efficient, and civic; its violence is often administrative before it is physical.

### Scavenger and informal networks

Informal crews and brokers exist in the wider campaign, but dynamic black markets, reputation simulation, and faction-economy systems are not Level 0 pillars or current MVP commitments.

## 11. Tone and dialogue

- Grounded dystopia and institutional paranoia, not gothic fantasy.
- Short, precise, conversational writing.
- Humor as survival instinct: dry, human, and never stronger than the stakes.
- Bureaucratic language should be frightening because it is calm, polished, and plausible.
- Corporate optimism and public-service language should contrast with what systems actually do.
- Distinct voices come from role, knowledge, and personal stakes—not procedural tone mixing.
- Avoid lore dumps. Each Level 0 line should support immediate understanding, character, decision, or consequence.
- Use Ukrainian localization as a full equivalent experience, not decorative slang. The broader cultural/language policy for Tokyo signage remains open.

Example tone, not production dialogue:

- **Human:** “They call it a safety hold. The medicine is still gone.”
- **Hidzu:** “Identity continuity protects every resident. Remain available for verification.”
- **George:** “I know where the camera looked. I do not know what it inferred.”

## 12. Visual and audio motifs

- Cameras embedded in civic fixtures rather than mounted as videogame turrets.
- Public screens alternating transit information, safety notices, curated civic sentiment, and suppressed absence.
- Sodium practical light against cold institutional material.
- Technology cyan only where a system is active and connected.
- Crimson only when danger is real.
- Repeated identity frames, queue markings, verification tones, and polite warnings.
- Drone approach announced by a recognizable hum and directional presence before verification.
- Missing-person imagery and shipping/logistics language connect personal loss to Cold Iron without supernatural symbolism.

## 13. Narrative prohibitions

Level 0 does not contain or imply:

- a fixed protagonist named Trace or Operative;
- Courier/Cadet/Medic backgrounds;
- Ghost/Wire/Force identities;
- fantasy hacking, EMP powers, noise magic, or unexplained military competence;
- tactical combat as the narrative climax;
- AutoBattle;
- Naila and Brant as mandatory errands;
- trust points, faction meters, procedural storylets, witness gossip, or reputation as narrative authority;
- false Paranoia information or hallucinated evidence;
- Takahiro as a boss fight;
- a playable Miami area before Level 1 exists.

Rejected ideas remain documented in [[01 MVP/12 Game Design Decision Register]] and historical progress records.

## 14. Open narrative decisions

The following are intentionally unresolved and must be answered before final content production:

- protagonist’s Tokyo chronology and exact flagging incident;
- Takahiro Kobayashi’s title;
- Lira, Naila, and Brant biographies and prior relationships;
- medkit beneficiaries and confiscation event;
- logistics-site identity (`OPEN-NAR-015`) and manifest contents;
- fictional reason for the midnight deadline;
- George’s hardware/substrate;
- exact capture/interception fiction;
- district name and Tokyo cultural/language policy.

The authoritative question wording, recommended baseline, and implementation blockers are in [[01 MVP/14 Specification Review Queue]]. Implementers may not fill these gaps by inference.

## 15. Narrative acceptance

Level 0 narrative is ready for implementation only when:

1. A player can explain who Hidzu is, what Lira needs, why passage matters, and when the deadline occurs without reading external documentation.
2. The protagonist feels customizable and civilian-scale rather than like a fixed commando.
3. Naila and Brant change practical knowledge without becoming mandatory.
4. The optional manifest connects Hidzu, Eisenclave/Harrow, Cold Iron, and Miami without blocking the local human objective.
5. George distinguishes known fact, inference, and missing evidence.
6. The debrief reflects actual actions and costs.
7. Tokyo’s prologue adds a modern surveillance lens while preserving the 2036 American Cold Iron campaign.
8. All open narrative decisions above are either approved or explicitly deferred without leaving a required script ambiguous.
